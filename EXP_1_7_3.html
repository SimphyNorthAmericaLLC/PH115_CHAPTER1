Zero flux alone does not prove zero field.

In electrostatic equilibrium, mobile charges rearrange until the field in the metal is zero. Gauss’s law then agrees: a sphere in the metal encloses q + (−q) = 0, so its net flux is zero. For a general surface, nonzero incoming and outgoing fields can cancel in the flux integral.

Model caveat. Redistribution is a schematic animation, not a time-resolved electromagnetic calculation. During it, equilibrium field and flux readouts are withheld. Final charges and fields are exact for an ideal isolated spherical conductor with a centered point charge, no external field, and vacuum in the cavity and outside. The shell starts neutral and remains neutral; it is not grounded.

E(r) = q / (4πε₀r²) in the cavity and outside; E = 0 in the metal.

The radial formula applies away from the central point and the conductor boundaries. Positive E means radially outward; negative E means inward. At q = 0, the field is zero everywhere.
